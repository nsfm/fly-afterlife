## Core claim
> "the model predicted a continuum of interleg coordination patterns spanning the complete range of walking speeds"; experimental data "suggest that changes observed in interleg coordination are part of a speed-dependent spectrum."

No discrete tetrapod→tripod transition speed is given, because the paper's point is that there isn't one.

## Numbers
- **Swing duration treated as a speed-invariant constant: "the global mean of all legs as swing duration (31 ms)".**
- Stepping frequency relation: **f_step = V_body/s + 1/T_sw**, with **V_body = s / T_st** (s = step amplitude, T_st = stance duration, T_sw = swing duration).
- Ipsilateral phase offset φ_I: **≈0.2 at ~3 BL/s**, **≈0.4 at ~10 BL/s**, converging toward 0.5.
- Contralateral phase offset **φ_C = 0.5** at all speeds (the static-stability optimum).
- Tripod coordination corresponds to φ_I = 0.5, φ_C = 0.5.
- Experimental speeds examined in the main results: **3–10 BL s⁻¹**.
- Model's predicted theoretical maximum speed: **~15 BL s⁻¹**.
- Wave-gait example at **2.5 BL s⁻¹** corresponds to a **duty cycle of 5/6 ≈ 0.83**.
- Body-weight distribution used by the static stability model: **head 12.5%, thorax 31%, abdomen 45%, legs 11%, wings 0.5%**.
