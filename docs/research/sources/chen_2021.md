Card GM, Tuthill JC (2021). "Functional architecture of neural circuits for leg proprioception in

PMID 34637749, PMC8665017.
**VERIFIED** by reading the PMC full text. Data: Dryad doi:10.5061/dryad.rfj6q57bm; Zenodo 6410281.
(Author list beyond the first two is second-hand from indexing — I read the PMC body text, not the
full author block; exact page range 5163–5175 also second-hand.)

Method: optogenetic stimulation of a specific FeCO subtype + two-photon calcium imaging of candidate
second-order VNC neurons ("functional connectivity"), plus EM reconstruction in a FANC-style VNC
volume, plus *trans*-Tango anatomical tracing.

## The key addition to the FeCO taxonomy (VERIFIED)

**This paper adds a fourth FeCO subtype.** New intersectional split-Gal4 lines for club, claw and hook
revealed *"a new FeCO subtype that responds to tibia extension in a directionally tuned manner. The
projections of these neurons are slightly different from the flexion-tuned hook neurons; therefore we
refer to this new FeCO subtype as 'hook (extension)' and flexion-tuned hook neurons as
'hook (flexion)'."*

So the canonical subtype list from 2021 onward is **club, claw, hook-flexion, hook-extension** — four,
not three. Mamiya 2018 had seen the extension-selective phasic cluster in population imaging but had
no driver line for it; this paper provides one. Mamiya 2023 then places hook-extension in FeCO
group 2 (distal to the claw array, on a medial-tendon branch) and hook-flexion in group 3.

## Downstream cell counts (VERIFIED)

Postsynaptic cells in the VNC, counted two ways:

| FeCO subtype | *trans*-Tango labelled | functional connectivity |
|---|---|---|
| club | **216** | **147** |
| claw | **566** | **443** |
| hook (flexion) | **74** | **21** |
| hook (extension) | **197** | **89** |
| **total** | **1,053** | **700** |

The functional screen found *"~1/3 fewer postsynaptic cells than trans-Tango"*, and the authors state
**both are underestimates** (driver lines do not label all FeCO neurons; *trans*-Tango has limited
sensitivity). Details in their Table S1 (not inspected).

**Note the asymmetry**: claw has by far the largest downstream fan-out (566 / 443), several times
club's, despite claw and club being comparable in number peripherally. Position information is more
heavily read out than vibration.

Context numbers: *"The VNC is composed of about 20,000 neurons that develop from 34 hemilineages."*
They screened **27 LexA lines** across hemilineages overlapping FeCO axons.

## Second-order classes identified (VERIFIED)

*"we identified 8 classes of VNC neurons from 6 lineages that responded to optogenetic stimulation of
one or more subtypes of FeCO neurons: **8Aa; 8Ba; 9Ba; 10Ba; 13Ba; 13Bb; 19Aa; and 19Ab**."*

- **2 classes respond to club: 9Ba and 10Ba.**
- **The remaining 6 respond to claw** (and/or hook).
- Naming convention established here and worth adopting: hemilineage number + A/B + a lowercase letter
  for distinct classes within that hemilineage, e.g. `9Aa`, `9Ba`, `13Ba`, `13Bb`.

### 9Ba — bilateral vibration integration
- GABAergic, local to a segment; innervates the ipsilateral neuromere densely and sends one process
  across the midline.
- EM confirms **direct synapses from club axons, both ipsilateral and contralateral.**
- MLA (1 µM) **fully blocks** club→9Ba → purely cholinergic, no gap junctions.
- Responds to passive tibia movement **both ipsi- and contralateral**, and to **0.1 µm vibration at
  200–2000 Hz**, similar to the club population itself.
- Higher baseline when the tibia is held fully extended — attributed to vibration from the fly
  resisting passive extension, which is a nice caution about interpreting "tonic" signals.
- **No frequency map** in 9Ba, unlike 10Ba.

### 10Ba — cross-segmental vibration integration
- Excitatory (cholinergic). A 10Ba with its soma in T1 arborises in its own neuromere, crosses the
  midline and arborises in the **contralateral T2** neuromere.
- EM confirms dense club→10Ba synapses, **and 10Ba(T1L)→10Ba(T2R) synapses**.
- Stimulating club axons in T1L drives 10Ba in **T1L and T2R only** — a 6×6 functional connectivity
  map was measured across all leg/neuromere pairs.
- MLA **reduces but does not eliminate** the club→10Ba signal → *"mixed chemical and electrical
  signaling"*. Also, **time-to-peak of the 10Ba signal is significantly longer under MLA**, implying
  the electrical and chemical components have different time courses.
- Responds to **ipsilateral but not contralateral** tibia movement in T1 (passive movement at
  **180 °/s**), bidirectionally.
- A subset projects to the brain, arborising in the contralateral **AMMC**.

### Adaptation is inhibition-dependent (VERIFIED, directly relevant to encoder design)
- Prolonged optogenetic stimulation: **20 s** for 10Ba, **30 s** for the others.
- **10Ba adapts** during 20 s club stimulation; **13Ba does not** — its signal *"remained elevated
  during optogenetic stimulation of claw neurons over a period of 30 s"*.
- **Picrotoxin (10 µM) reduced response adaptation in 10Ba and 13Bb.** So adaptation in these
  second-order cells is **circuit-level, produced by GABA_A / GluCl inhibition — it is not intrinsic
  to the sensory neurons.**
- Adaptation index defined as `1 − F_late/F_early` (1 = complete adaptation, 0 = none, negative =
  facilitation). Per-cell-class values are figure-only — **could not verify numeric adaptation
  indices.**
- Anatomical segregation: 9Ba and 10Ba arborise in the **mVAC** (medial ventral association centre),
  supporting a split between a vibration/exteroceptive pathway and a position/movement motor-feedback
  pathway.
