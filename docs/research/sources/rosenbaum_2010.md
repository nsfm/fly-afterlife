# Rosenbaum, Wosnitza, Büschges & Gruhn 2010 — **(C) stick insect, not Drosophila**

**Citation.** Rosenbaum P, Wosnitza A, Büschges A, Gruhn M (2010). "Activity patterns and timing of muscle activity in the forward walking and backward walking stick insect *Carausius morosus*." *Journal of Neurophysiology* **104**(3):1681–1695. DOI 10.1152/jn.00362.2010. PMID 20668273. Epub 2010 Jul 28.
Verified from the NCBI eutils record (full abstract retrieved verbatim).

**Method.** EMG from three antagonist pairs of *middle leg* muscles — protractor/retractor coxae, levator/depressor trochanteris, extensor/flexor tibiae — with precise **electrical** measurement of tarsus touchdown and liftoff. Comparison of intact animals with reduced two-leg and one-middle-leg preparations.

## The table everyone imports

| Muscle | Phase (forward walking) | First-spike latency |
|---|---|---|
| depressor trochanteris | **stance** | **93 ms before touchdown** |
| flexor tibiae | **stance** | **9 ms after touchdown** |
| retractor coxae | **stance** | **35 ms after touchdown** |
| levator trochanteris | **swing** | **100 ms before liftoff** |
| extensor tibiae | **swing** | **67 ms before liftoff** |
| protractor coxae | **swing** | **37 ms before liftoff** |

Verbatim from the abstract:
> "Forward walking stance phase muscle (depressor, flexor, and retractor) activities were tightly coupled to touchdown, beginning on average 93 ms prior to and 9 and 35 ms after touchdown, respectively. Forward walking swing phase muscle (levator, extensor, and protractor) activities were less tightly coupled to liftoff, beginning on average 100, 67, and 37 ms before liftoff, respectively."

## Backward walking
> "In backward walking the protractor/retractor muscles reversed their phasing compared with forward walking, with the retractor being active during swing and the protractor during stance."

Levator/depressor and extensor/flexor keep their forward-walking phasing. So the **coxal pair carries walking direction; the distal pairs carry the step cycle**.

## Other results
- Reduced preparations: "only small alterations in overall EMG activity but changes in first spike latencies in most muscles."
- Changing body height (⇒ changing leg-joint loading) "altered the intensity, but not the timing, of depressor muscle activity."

## Caveat for importing into a fly model
Stick insect step cycles are roughly an order of magnitude slower than fly cycles. A **93 ms** pre-touchdown lead is longer than a fly's entire swing phase (~30 ms). **Scale these latencies as fractions of the cycle, not in milliseconds.** Also note this table contradicts measured fly kinematics for two of the three leg pairs (Haustein 2024: fly front legs extend in swing and flex in stance; hind legs do the reverse), so "flexor = stance" is not a safe blanket rule in *Drosophila*.
